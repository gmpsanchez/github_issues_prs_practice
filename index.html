Facts About Glasgow

  • It's a UNESCO City of Music
  • It has the third-oldest subway system in the world
  • The ultrasound was invented here in 1956
  • Oasis got their big break after playing here in 1993
  • There is always a traffic cone on the Duke of Wellington statue!
  • Glasgow's name comes from a Gaelic phrase often interpreted as “dear green place.”
  • The Kelvingrove Art Gallery and Museum opened in 1901.
  • The Glasgow School of Art was designed by renowned Scottish architect Charles Rennie Mackintosh.
  • The River Clyde helped Glasgow become a major shipbuilding centre during the Industrial Revolution.
  • - Glasgow is home to the Burrell Collection, a major art collection gifted to the city by Sir William Burrell.

Facts About Manchester

  • Home of the World Pie-Eating Championships
  • The first passenger railway train set of from here in 1830
  • The first computer with stored program and memory is located here
  • The Suffragette movement started here
  • The vegetarian movement started here in 1847
  • Manchester's Free Trade Hall was built to commemorate the repeal of the Corn Laws in 1846.
  • The Manchester Museum houses a famous collection of Egyptian mummies and ancient artefacts.
  • The first meeting of the Football League took place in Manchester in 1888, at the Royal Hotel.
  • Manchester's Central Library is inspired by the Pantheon in Rome.
  • The city played a major role in the Industrial Revolution, particularly in cotton manufacturing.

Facts About Birmingham

  • There are more canals here (35 miles) than in Venice (26 miles)
  • The world's first football league started here
  • The postage stamp was invented here in 1839
  • Home to the 5th busiest McDonald's in the world
  • The board game Cluedo was invented here in 1943
  • J.R.R. Tolkien spent part of his childhood in the Birmingham area, and local landscapes influenced his imagination.
  • The city is home to the Barber Institute of Fine Arts, known for its collection of European paintings and sculptures.
  • Cadbury's chocolate factory opened in Bournville, Birmingham, in 1879.
  • Birmingham was a major centre for jewellery-making and is still home to the historic Jewellery Quarter.
  • - The Lunar Society, a famous group of scientists and inventors, met in Birmingham and the surrounding area during the 18th century.

Facts About London

  • The oldest building in London is almost 1800 years old!
  • Holds the record for most Olympic Games hosted by a city
  • The shortest street is only 36ft long
  • Has so many trees that it meets the UN's definition of a forest
  • London is home to Big Ben, but Big Ben is actually the name of the Great Bell inside the Elizabeth Tower at the Houses of Parliament, not the tower itself!
  • The London Underground opened in 1863, making it the world's oldest underground railway.
  • Tower Bridge opened in 1894 and was originally painted a chocolate-brown colour.
  • The Tower of London was founded by William the Conqueror in the 11th century.
  • The Shard stands approximately 310 metres tall, making it one of London's most recognisable skyscrapers.
  • London's famous Notting Hill Carnival celebrates Caribbean culture and is one of Europe's largest street festivals.

Facts About Cape Town

  • Winemaking in the region dates back to 1659 - the oldest vineyards outside Europe!
  • Hosted the first FIFA World Cup match held in Africa in 2010
  • Home to a colony of endangered penguins
  • Has 7000 species of plant which aren't found anywhere else in the world
  • The world's first heart transplant took place here in 1967
  • Table Mountain is recognised as one of the New7Wonders of Nature.
  • The Castle of Good Hope, built in the 17th century, is the oldest surviving colonial building in South Africa.
  • Robben Island, near Cape Town, is where Nelson Mandela was imprisoned for many years.
  • Cape Town is home to Kirstenbosch National Botanical Garden, famous for showcasing South Africa's native plants.
  • - The colourful Bo-Kaap neighbourhood is known for its brightly painted houses and rich Cape Malay cultural heritage.
